## Design Context

### Users
- **Shoppers in south Trinidad, two generations at once.** Older customers who shop in person, read on a phone held at arm's length, and want hours, prices and "which branch" without hunting. Millennials who follow the store on Instagram, know the "1 FOR $x · TRY ME" posts, and check a deal before they drive.
- **Job seekers** applying for floor, bakery, warehouse and office roles, often on a phone, often for their first formal job.
- **Staff and HR** using the employee portal and HR console on shared desktops and their own phones.
- Jobs to be done: see the current deals, find the nearest branch and when it is open, apply for a job with the right documents, check a shift, payslip or leave balance.

### Brand Personality
- Three words: **neighbourly, festive, dependable.**
- The physical object it should feel like: the store's own wave-striped flags on the music truck, and a printed supermarket flyer with scalloped price seals. Not a tech start-up, not a luxury grocer.
- Emotional goal: "this is my store" for the 65-year-old and "they're actually good at this" for the 30-year-old.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Traditional, warm, bright, light theme.** Built from the real brand: the cornucopia logo, raspberry pink and leaf green wordmark, fruit orange, staff-polo red, and the yellow price pill from their posts.
- Real assets only: the logo and product photos come from the company's own Instagram (supplied by Gregg on 2026-10-23). No stock, no generated food.
- Brand-sourced ornament: the pink-and-green wave from their flags (section dividers, header edge) and the scalloped red "1 FOR / TRY ME" seal (prices).
- Type: **Caprasimo** (Cooper-style soft serif that echoes the wordmark) for headings, **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** for everything people must read, **Alfa Slab One** only for prices inside seals (echoes the flyer numerals).
- Anti-references: the first concept's quiet forest-green "premium grocer" look (too modern for this audience), dark mode, glassmorphism, gradient text, side-stripe callouts, identical icon-card grids, tiny grey text.

### Design Principles
1. **Read it at arm's length.** 17 px base text, 18–20 px on public pages, strong contrast, labelled buttons ("Menu", not just an icon), no meaning carried by colour alone.
2. **Look like their store, not like a template.** Every colour, ornament and photo traces to something the business already uses.
3. **Prices are loud, everything else is calm.** Seals and pills are the only shouting on the page; the rest is generous whitespace and plain sections.
4. **Familiar structure.** Utility bar with hours, logo masthead, a solid nav band, flyer grid, store list, footer with every branch. People should find things where they expect them.
5. **Honest by default.** Demo data is labelled where it appears; real prices carry their post date; the concept banner never hides.
