## Supplier specials, from a message to the shelf list

Department: Administration. What each company sends becomes one checked table, with nothing typed twice.

1. **Open a book** (Administration): One book for each round: Divali, Christmas, or a plain month. Old books are kept, so nothing is ever overwritten to save space.
2. **Bring it in** (Administration): Upload the whole Excel workbook, one company’s file, or paste the text of a WhatsApp message or e-mail.
3. **System reads it** (The system): Items, counts, specials and dates are filled into the six columns the office already uses. Each company gets its own sheet.
4. **Add case price** (Administration): The one cell companies leave off. Type it in and the unit cost and the margin at the special appear by themselves.
5. **Star key lines** (Administration): Starred lines gather on the key specials sheet. Nobody copies them across by hand.
6. **Mark checked** (Administration): A person confirms every sheet. Marketing then draws on the checked list for posts and the website.

## Sell-out reports, owed to sent

Department: Administration. Which companies want units sold after a special, and where each report stands.

1. **Special runs** (The stores): A company that asks for sell-out reports has specials in the book.
2. **Report owed** (The system): When the round has their lines in it, a report shows as owed. Nothing is forgotten in a notebook.
3. **Record units** (Administration): The lines come from their sheet. Type the units sold; the value at the special adds itself up.
4. **Attach the page** (Administration): A photo of the notebook page or till print-out rides along as the record.
5. **Send it** (Administration): Mark it sent once the company has it. The list shows what is still owed at a glance.

## A request, from the letter to the handover

Department: Administration. Schools, charities, clubs and companies ask; one pipeline carries each request to a decision.

1. **They ask** (A school, charity, club or company): Through the form on the public site, or by letter, WhatsApp or a visit that the office logs. Every request gets a reference.
2. **Office picks it up** (Administration): The office gets what is missing and checks with the store it concerns.
3. **Director decides** (Managing Director): Approve or decline on the card, with what is being given and a note. No chat thread to scroll back through.
4. **Reply goes out** (Administration): The reply is written on the card and e-mailed. Notes between staff stay on the same thread, marked as internal.
5. **Handed over** (The store): Marked done when the goods leave or the event is held. The year’s total gives adds up by itself.

## From the designer’s desk to the customer’s phone

Department: Marketing. Flyers, events and promotions go on the public site the moment they are saved.

1. **Flyers in** (Graphic designer): Drop the week’s finished flyers in, several at once. They are made light enough for phones and lead the Specials page.
2. **Specials in** (Marketing or Administration): Paste the week’s price list and every special is added in one go, each in its department.
3. **On now** (The system): An event shows on the Events page from its first day to its last. Nobody has to remember to take it down.
4. **Coming up** (Marketing): Plan ahead: a date, one sentence, up to three points, and the stores it is at.

## Customer feedback, from the button to the answer

Department: Administration. A shopper taps Feedback on the website; customer service answers and closes it in one place.

1. **Customer writes** (A shopper): The Feedback button sits at the bottom right of every public page: a compliment, a suggestion, a question or a complaint, with the store it is about.
2. **Ticket arrives** (The system): It gets a reference, the customer gets an acknowledgement, and it appears in the office’s list. Complaints sit at the top.
3. **Someone picks it up** (Customer service): One named person looks after it, with notes for staff kept on the same card.
4. **Reply goes out** (Customer service): The reply is written on the card and e-mailed, or the call is recorded. What the customer says back is kept too.
5. **Resolved** (Customer service): Closed with a line on how it ended, so the same problem can be counted, not remembered.

## One bulk order for five stores

Department: Administration. Turkeys, butter cookies, oil: each store gives a number, one order is placed, the record stays.

1. **Open the order** (Managing Director or Administration): The item, the company, the unit and its cost, and the day the stores must answer by.
2. **Stores answer** (Each store manager): Each manager types their store’s number. Last time’s figure sits beside it, so nobody orders blind.
3. **Order placed** (Managing Director): The total and the cost add up as the numbers come in. Place it once the stores have answered.
4. **Received** (Administration): Marked received when the stock reaches the warehouse.
5. **Shared out** (Administration): Marked shared when it leaves for the stores. This order becomes “last time” for next year.

## Handing work over

Department: Administration. Who, what and by when, plus the letters and checklists the office reuses.

1. **Hand it out** (Managing Director or Administration): Give a task to a person with a date.
2. **It reaches them** (The system): It shows on their Today page until it is done, and turns red when it is late.
3. **Ticked off** (Whoever it was given to): They tick it off. Whoever handed it out sees it done.
4. **Reuse the text** (Everyone in the office): Reply letters and checklists are kept as resources: open, copy, or change for everyone.

## From a post to the till

Department: Marketing. The accounts feed one page of figures, so the team can see what worked.

1. **Post goes out** (Marketing): A video, photo or carousel on Instagram, TikTok or Facebook, planned on the content calendar.
2. **Figures fetched** (The system): Each night the connected accounts hand over views, reach, likes, comments and shares. The history builds up here.
3. **One page** (Marketing): Four figures answer “are we growing”; the chart shows where; the table ranks the posts that did it.
4. **Codes at the till** (The stores): Offer codes taken online and used at a till are counted by the system, so one figure reaches the shop floor.
5. **Do more of it** (Marketing): “What the figures say” puts the main points in plain words for the next plan.

## Connecting an account

Department: Marketing. A one-time, read-only sign-in on the platform’s own page.

1. **Owner signs in** (Whoever owns the account): On that platform’s own page. The password never passes through this system.
2. **Read-only** (The platform): They approve access to the figures only. The system cannot post, message or change anything.
3. **Nightly fetch** (The system): New figures arrive each night and are kept, even though the platforms only show recent weeks.
4. **On the page** (Marketing): The account’s line joins the chart. Disconnect at any time from here or from the platform.

## The content calendar

Department: Marketing. Posts, e-mails and website changes on the day they go out.

1. **Idea** (Marketing): Put each post, e-mail and website change on the day it should go out.
2. **Planned** (Marketing): Marked planned once it is ready, with where it is going.
3. **Posted** (Marketing): Marked posted when it is out. It then shows up in the figures.
4. **Site in step** (Marketing): Website changes sit here too, so a seasonal look goes live the same day as the posts about it.

## Dressing the website for a season

Department: Website. A seasonal look is a layer: it goes on and comes off without touching the content.

1. **Choose a look** (Marketing): Christmas, Divali, or the everyday site. Each look changes colours and adds ornament over the same pages.
2. **Preview** (Marketing): See it on the real home page before anyone else does, on a phone-sized or full-sized screen.
3. **Set the dates** (Marketing): Put it on now, or give it a first and last day so it starts and ends by itself.
4. **Live** (The system): Every public page wears the look. Switching it off puts the site back exactly as it was.

## Hiring, from the advert to day one

Department: HR. One application form, a document checklist, and a decision delivered with care.

1. **Job opens** (HR): HR opens a role for a store. It appears on the careers page straight away.
2. **They apply** (The applicant): One form on a phone, with the documents the role needs. It saves as they go.
3. **Documents checked** (HR): Each document is verified, or flagged with a reason the applicant sees and can fix.
4. **Interview** (HR and the store manager): Booked from the application, with the place and the person to ask for.
5. **Decision** (HR): The applicant gets “an update”, and opens a letter written with care, whichever way it went.
6. **Joins the team** (HR): A hire moves to People with their documents, and gets a first-week checklist.

## Looking after the team

Department: HR. Everyone at all five stores, their documents and training, and what is about to expire.

1. **One list** (HR): Every person at every store, by store and by team, with who is on probation or on leave.
2. **Documents** (The system): Food badges and IDs carry expiry dates. The ones running out are flagged before they lapse.
3. **Leave** (Employees and HR): People ask for leave in their portal; HR approves and the balance updates.
4. **Resources** (HR): Checklists, the interview guide and review forms are kept to hand as HR resources.

## What reaches the owner

Department: Managing Director. Every department on one page, with the decisions only the owner can make at the top.

1. **Departments work** (Administration, Marketing, HR): Each department works in its own area and sees only what its job needs.
2. **Decisions surface** (The system): A request to approve, an offer to sign off, an order ready to place: they rise to the top of the owner’s page.
3. **Director decides** (Managing Director): One click from the overview to the card, and the decision is recorded with who made it and when.
4. **Everything logged** (The system): Every change by every person is in the activity log, with refused attempts beside it.

## Signing in

Department: Everyone. A password, then a code on the phone, and access that matches the job.

1. **Work e-mail** (Each member of staff): Everyone has their own sign-in. Nobody shares one.
2. **Password** (Each member of staff): Checked by the sign-in service, never stored by the website itself.
3. **Code on the phone** (The system): A six-digit code confirms it is really them, each time on a new device.
4. **Their own access** (Managing Director): They land on their own dashboard. Access is given by job, limited to stores, and can be ended at once.
